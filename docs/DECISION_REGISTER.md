The user values independent modules but wants miners to experience convenient, simple access. ## 3. Worker access — confirmed direction

CONFIRMED D-001: Ordinary workers should be able to look up approved chemical information and view the actual SDS without individual login or routine cellular service. SDS access is read-only. Design for worker speed, simple navigation, exact chemical identity, and no invented AI-generated SDS/hazard substitutions.

CONFIRMED D-002: Support three lookup modes: product/manufacturer/name search; QR-code scan; browsing by mine site and work area. Provide a manual name/product-ID fallback when QR scanning or a network link fails. QR is a supplemental shortcut and must not replace required chemical hazard labels.

CONFIRMED D-003: Support both optional personal worker phones and company-owned shared devices/computers. Personal-device access is a practical startup bridge; move toward company-issued devices as operations grow. No miner should be required to provide a personal smartphone for required SDS access. Plan paper binder/shared-device fallback and accessibility when devices are missing, discharged or broken.

CONFIRMED D-004: Offline capability matters. Appropriately published SDS documents must be synchronized/downloaded before connectivity is lost, and a device should show its last successful sync. If an SDS is not cached, say so rather than promising offline availability. A QR code alone does not provide data offline. Design an appropriate update/reconciliation process and printed backup.

## 4. SDS lifecycle and administration

CONFIRMED D-005: Administrators can create/revise a chemical record, upload the exact original manufacturer SDS, associate it with relevant site/work areas, review and publish updates, and retain controlled document history. Avoid silently replacing content without traceability. The worker-facing approved library should not expose unreviewed submissions as if they were verified SDS records.

CONFIRMED D-006: Printing is a first-class feature. Allow printing original SDS PDFs, printable product QR-code labels, and organized site/work-area SDS binder packages or indexes for physical backup. Use stable product IDs/links rather than encoding a particular PDF revision in the QR code, so updated approved SDSs do not require every code to be replaced.

CONFIRMED D-007: Management needs an actionable gap dashboard: missing SDSs, product/label mismatches, new document revisions requiring review, unassigned/uncertain work area, duplicate or unidentified products, and unresolved worker reports. Each gap should lead to its underlying record and next review action. Do not label an SDS 'expired' based solely on age or claim that an overall green dashboard proves legal compliance.

WORKING D-008: Distinguish 'SDS/product document verified' from 'chemical approved for use by the mine.' The company's chemical-purchasing/use approval process and authority still require operator leadership decisions. Drafting a record, possessing an SDS, and approving chemical use are not equivalent states.

**CONFIRMED D-022 (2026-11-22) — Mandatory administrator safety triage for newly reported chemicals.** When a new-chemical report enters the administrator review queue, an authorized reviewer must complete and record initial safety triage **before routine product/SDS verification**. The triage captures reported use status (**currently being used**, **present/stored but not known to be in use**, or **unknown—field verification needed**) and considers potential worker exposure and whether immediate supervisor/Safety communication or field follow-up is needed. An unknown use status is not evidence of no exposure. For potentially urgent hazards the interface must direct timely real-world notification/escalation, rather than leave workers relying only on a digital report. Keep an auditable triage outcome associated with the report. This is a **software workflow requirement**, not a decision about mine-wide chemical-use approval, stop-work thresholds, or escalation ownership; those operator procedures remain **OPEN** under Q-002 and Q-004. **Rationale:** identify active exposure risks early while keeping SDS document review distinct from operational hazard response and authorization to use a chemical.

**CONFIRMED D-023 (2026-11-22) — One controlled master product record per verified manufacturer/product identity, with separate site and work-area assignments.** When a worker reports a chemical, the administrator compares manufacturer, exact product name, product identifiers and available label evidence with existing records, including records associated with another mine site. A verified same-product match reuses the existing controlled master product record and its SDS version history rather than creating another master product record solely because the product is present at another site. Each site/work-area maintains its own assignment and independent operational authorization/use status; a verified SDS or record at Spirit Mountain does **not** automatically approve use at Quartette. Similar names, labels or potentially different formulations are only **possible matches**, never automatically merged; uncertain identity stays in a pending-review state. **Rationale:** avoid document duplication, keep revisions traceable across sites and preserve local operational control. The precise matching fields, reviewer authority and approval policy remain part of OPEN Q-002 and Q-006.

**CONFIRMED D-024 (2026-11-22) — One authorized administrator can verify an SDS for publication.** Before an SDS enters the approved worker-facing document library, one authorized administrator must document verification that the complete, unmodified manufacturer SDS corresponds to the exact identified product, using available manufacturer name, product name and identifiers/label evidence. Preserve the source SDS and document-control metadata (source, available issue/revision information, verification date and responsible reviewer) and retain version history rather than silently overwriting. If identity or document authenticity is unresolved, mark the submission pending clarification and do not present it as a verified published SDS. A **second administrator approval is not routinely required** to publish a successfully verified SDS. Whether particular discrepancies or significant revisions should trigger an additional reviewer is **OPEN**, not approved as an automatic rule. Document verification does not grant chemical-use authorization; operator approval authority remains OPEN under Q-002. **Rationale:** maintain traceability and product accuracy without an unnecessary mandatory two-person review bottleneck.

**CONFIRMED D-025 (2026-11-22) — Verified SDS access is independent of mine-specific chemical-use authorization.** The worker-facing library may show a complete, verified manufacturer SDS even when site-specific permission to use that chemical is unconfirmed. Clearly and separately identify the **document verification status** and the **site/work-area use-authorization status** so a worker never interprets "SDS verified" or "SDS published" as "chemical approved for use." A verified SDS must remain available for hazard-information access; uncertain use authority is shown as unconfirmed and flagged for administrator follow-up. Actual company chemical-use approval decisions and any stop-work procedure remain with mine leadership under OPEN Q-002. **Rationale:** prevent loss of essential hazard information while avoiding accidental authorization through UI wording.

**CONFIRMED D-026 (2026-11-22) — Quick worker report of observed chemical use or incorrect site status.** On a worker-visible chemical/SDS record, provide an obvious, low-friction action to report that the chemical **is being used, has appeared at a work area, or has an incorrect/unconfirmed status** and request administrator review. Where known, prefill the exact product and site/work area; allow brief optional context, with the submission routed into a trackable administrative review queue. This is **a report/request only**, not worker authority to change an official use-approval status or to authorize continued use. If reported use could indicate an unapproved or potentially hazardous condition, route it to the mandatory safety triage/escalation process in D-022; urgent hazards still require direct real-world notification. Minimize required steps while honoring the unresolved authenticated reporting/shared-device and offline-delivery rules in D-012/D-013. **Rationale:** catch real-world chemical use and stale site records without placing an unrealistic recordkeeping burden on miners. Exact report fields, identity mechanism, urgency threshold and downstream administrative status values remain OPEN/WORKING, not frozen by this decision.

**CONFIRMED D-027 (2026-11-22) — Two-action worker chemical-use/status reporting.** From an approved worker-visible chemical/SDS record, a worker can initiate a status review by (1) selecting **“Report chemical in use”** (or equivalent context-specific status-report action), then (2) selecting **“Submit”** on a compact confirmation view. Prefill the product and known site/work area and permit correction of the location before submitting; short notes and photos are optional, not required for routine reporting. On successful server receipt, create a traceable administrator-review report and acknowledge receipt to the worker. This action reports **observed chemical use/presence or a suspected incorrect status**; it must not directly alter official product, assignment, or use-authorization states, imply the chemical is approved to use, or replace immediate field notification for an urgent hazard. Maintain the prior D-012/D-013 boundaries: individual report attribution, shared-device identity, and offline saved-versus-received handling remain **WORKING/OPEN** and must be resolved before implementation. **Rationale:** maximize practical worker participation while allowing an administrator to verify actual use and investigate authorization or safety gaps. This approval does not settle operator policy, reviewer permissions, or offline reporting mechanics.

**CONFIRMED D-028 (2026-11-22) — Verify worker-reported chemical use before changing official inventory or authorization records.** Once a worker submits an observed-use or site-status report (D-026/D-027), the administrator may immediately record the finding as **“Observed use — pending verification”** and begin safety triage (D-022). The report remains distinct from the official chemical inventory/site assignment and chemical-use authorization status until the administrator verifies whether the product is actually present or in use at the specified location. Only after sufficient verification may an authorized administrator update the appropriate inventory/assignment records. Even after confirmed use, a change to **authorized for use** requires the mine operator’s own approval process; observation or inventory verification does not grant permission to use the chemical. Record the verification and disposition in the report audit trail, retaining unresolved reports for follow-up. **Rationale:** capture frontline observations promptly while preventing erroneous inventory updates or accidental chemical-use authorization. The precise field-verification evidence standard, chemical-use approval authority, and escalation routes remain OPEN for mine leadership and detailed workflow discovery.

**CONFIRMED D-029 (2026-11-22) — Documented administrative resolution and reporter notification, with independent follow-up for unresolved authorization.** Each worker report of chemical use/status requires a documented administrator investigation outcome before the report is marked resolved. The minimum proposed outcomes accepted for the workflow are **confirmed in use** (update verified site/work-area inventory only after D-028 verification), **not found** (document what was checked), **incorrect product identified** (record correction and any required identity/SDS re-verification), and **further investigation needed** (remain open/pending, not falsely counted as resolved). Preserve the finding, reviewer, date, actions and report audit history. When a report is actually resolved, the reporting worker receives an **automatic, concise status update** via an appropriate channel to be designed; do not assume push delivery, real-time connectivity, or that a notification proves training. If use is confirmed but site-specific use authorization is still unknown/unapproved, preserve a **separately trackable, unresolved authorization concern** with ownership and follow-up rather than treating closure of the original report as closure of the safety/authorization issue. Such concern must not silently authorize use or become a parallel general work-management system; any eventual referral to Lynx remains an uncommitted integration. **Rationale:** visible closeout for workers, defensible record history, and no loss of unresolved chemical-use issues. Exact disposition labels, notification channel, follow-up owner, escalation deadlines and operator authority remain OPEN for later workflow/policy decisions.

## 5. Worker gap reports and discrepancy review

CONFIRMED D-009: Workers should be able to identify gaps, mark an existing SDS or record for administrator follow-up, or report a new chemical missing from the library. Two explicit new-chemical paths: (A) supply basic product/manufacturer/location details, evidence, optional SDS and submit a proposed chemical entry for review; (B) flag an unknown or missing chemical with whatever information is available and let an administrator identify/add it. Either way the item enters an administrator review queue, not the published worker catalog.

CONFIRMED D-010: Workers may attach product/label photographs and video recordings, with optional context and work area. Reports should have an ID, date/status, assigned review owner, ability to request clarification, resolution record, and change/audit history. Photos/videos may capture sensitive people, equipment and sites, so plan controlled media access, size/retention rules and a nonpublic storage design.

CONFIRMED D-011: Ordinary workers should NOT see every pending/unverified chemical proposal or administrative edit. Approved chemical records/SDSs are available to everyone; pending submissions/discrepancies are visible to the submitting worker, authorized administrators and designated reviewers/supervisors. The worker can track their own report, and relevant safety warnings are communicated separately if immediate hazard information is needed. Do not let hidden review queues suppress necessary direct hazard communication.

WORKING D-012: Reporting is tied to a signed-in individual worker so name/contact can be captured automatically without redundant entry. SDS lookup remains login-free. Shared-device sessions must identify the actual reporting worker rather than attributing a report to the prior user. Authentication/enrollment specifics have NOT been chosen; shared identity with SkyFire Education is only a possible future integration.

WORKING D-013: Offline drafting of a discrepancy report may be supported, but it must display 'saved locally — not yet received' until the server confirms submission. A pending offline report cannot substitute for immediate supervisor/Safety notification of a serious hazard.

## 6. Notifications and workforce communication

CONFIRMED D-014: The user wants a notice system that alerts administrators to new chemical submissions/SDS discrepancies and can notify affected workers of newly added chemicals, important hazard changes or SDS updates when warranted. They also want to explore opt-in push notifications and perhaps app-wide notices. Notifications must be relevant rather than broadcasting every minor administrative edit.

WORKING D-015: Suggested hierarchy: administrator action alerts; important chemical hazard/SDS changes targeting affected areas or the mine as appropriate; routine publication/update notices in an in-app center or digest. Store notice history and read/acknowledgment metadata where legitimately needed. A notification is not proof that required training or other communication occurred; possible referral/assignment through SkyFire Education is future integration.

DEFERRED D-016: Push notifications are a later-phase candidate due to permissions, device/platform support, hosting, and inability to deliver fresh push alerts to disconnected devices. An in-app notification center and administrator review queue are preferred initial targets, subject to requirements review.

**CONFIRMED D-030 (2026-11-22) — Targeted chemical notifications by affected site/work area by default.** Notices about new verified SDSs or materially changed chemical hazards should ordinarily target workers in the relevant mine sites and work areas, rather than sending every minor update to the entire workforce. Authorized administrators may broaden the audience to more areas, an entire site, or broader relevant groups when the safety context warrants it. Design separate notification classes/audiences for supervisors and ordinary workers where their actions differ; specific triggers, urgency and acknowledgment criteria remain OPEN. Notifications supplement, never replace, required in-person hazard communication, training, or urgent response. **Rationale:** relevant alerts without notification fatigue, while preserving administrator discretion to widen important warnings.

**CONFIRMED D-031 (2026-11-22) — Organization-aware user profiles, supervisor hierarchy and delegated supervisor administration as product direction.** For users who sign in for reporting, work-group notices or protected functions, plan profile associations with **mine site, department/work area, role, and supervisory/reporting relationship** so affected-worker and supervisor audiences can be selected and differentiated. Provide supervisors with actionable notices relevant to their teams or areas, not merely copies of all worker alerts. The product owner wants authorized administrators to be able to empower supervisors with **selected administrative controls**, rather than assume every supervisor gets unrestricted write access merely from their job title. Preserve D-001: viewing approved SDSs remains **login-free**; organizational enrollment must not become an SDS-access prerequisite. Minimize personal data visibility and respect multi-site/work-area membership. **Rationale:** support responsibility-based communication and operational response as a mine grows. Precise role definitions, which admin powers may be delegated, scope, grant authority, enrollment and exact employee hierarchy remain design questions, not mine policy.

**CONFIRMED D-032 (2026-11-22) — Three base roles with individually configurable, scoped supervisor permissions.** The product owner approved the overall three-role design: **Field User**, **Supervisor**, and **Admin**, rather than an all-or-nothing administrative elevation for supervisors. Supervisors have a distinct team/area-oriented role and can receive **specific administrator-granted SDS application controls** on a least-privilege basis, limited to authorized sites/work areas where appropriate. Delegations must be auditable and revocable, not automatically inherited as full Admin privileges because someone is a supervisor. This design maintains D-001 login-free access to approved SDSs; sign-in applies to reporting/personalized notifications or protected operations as scoped in further design. **The approval is for role architecture and granular delegation, not a final permissions matrix or an operator policy.** **OPEN:** default Supervisor capabilities, exact grantable actions, who may grant/revoke, multi-site scope, reporting hierarchy edge cases, enrollment/authentication, coverage and escalation, and whether certain high-risk actions should be Admin-only. SDS-app privileges must never be confused with independent mine authority to approve chemicals for use. **History:** Originally recorded earlier on 2026-11-22 as WORKING D-032; advanced to CONFIRMED after owner approval the same day.

**CONFIRMED D-033 (2026-11-22) — Supervisor default visibility, acknowledgment and escalation without default record-edit rights.** The Supervisor base role should provide visibility into chemical reports/unresolved concerns relevant to assigned teams and work areas; supervisor-specific actionable notifications; ability to acknowledge reports, add investigation context and escalate issues; and ordinary access to verified SDSs. A supervisor's job title by itself does **not** authorize modifying controlled SDS documents, publishing revisions, changing authoritative inventory/site assignments or changing site chemical-use authorization. SDS application record-editing controls require specific permission grants under D-032/D-034, while any operational authority to approve chemical use remains a separate mine-operator decision. **Rationale:** empower first-line supervision without silently granting broad administrative powers. **OPEN:** exact visibility scope, confidentiality boundaries and permission matrix.

**CONFIRMED D-034 (2026-11-22) — Individual, scoped permission increases and restrictions across all three base roles.** The SDS product must let appropriately authorized administrators **increase, narrow, revoke or temporarily extend individual users' app permissions**, not just grant privileges to supervisors. This includes Field Users, Supervisors, and Admin accounts, subject to a yet-to-be-defined privileged-grant authority boundary; an ordinary Admin must not be assumed able to elevate themselves or override higher-level security controls. Example needs include temporary cross-department Supervisor coverage and proportionate restriction of a Field User's misused nonessential reporting features. Preserve role-based defaults, but support user-specific exceptions, site/department/work-area scopes, and a clear audit trail of who changed what and why. Temporary access should be supported without granting whole-system Admin, with expiration handling to be decided. **Rationale:** operational coverage, security and flexibility without proliferation of roles. **OPEN / safety and regulatory QA:** precise adjustable permissions, limits on changing Admin privileges, approval/escalation for restrictive actions, temporary-access expiry defaults, and cross-site privilege inheritance. Permission restrictions must never block login-free access to approved SDSs (D-001) or suppress genuine/protected safety reporting; flagging alleged erroneous reports for independent review is preferable to disabling a miner's hazard-reporting channel. For legal review, 30 CFR § 47.51 says electronic SDS media must not restrict availability, and Mine Act § 105(c) protects safety complaints: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/30/47.51 and https://www.dol.gov/general/topics/whistleblower . This is product design, not authorization to make disciplinary or mine-wide reporting policy.

**CONFIRMED D-035 (2026-11-22) — Time-limited grants expire automatically; justified permanent grants remain available.** Authorized administrators may issue user-specific permission grants for an explicitly identified person, allowed action(s), and permitted mine site/department/work-area scope. A grant marked **temporary** requires a definite expiration date/time and must automatically cease granting privileges when it expires, without unintentionally withdrawing separate valid baseline or independent grants. An administrator can also deliberately make an appropriate grant **permanent**, with a recorded justification, rather than requiring every exception to expire. Keep audit history of the granting actor, affected user, permission scope, reason, effective and expiration details and subsequent changes or revocation. This is a product capability, not authority for any particular administrator to give themselves greater privileges, to approve mine chemical use, or to curtail access to approved SDSs or protected safety complaints. **Rationale:** support temporary cross-department coverage and durable justified responsibilities without accumulating forgotten privileges. **OPEN:** who may grant/extend privileged permissions, approval limits, reminder/escalation policy, exact default duration (the previous seven-day example was illustrative only), timezone behavior, precedence of overlapping grants/restrictions, and how offline devices enforce revocations. D-034's earlier open note about whether temporary grants expire is resolved by D-035; other permission/governance questions remain open.

**CONFIRMED D-036 (2026-11-22) — Online authorization required for protected administrative changes; safety information remains available offline.** The SDS system must verify the user's **current** authorization with the trusted online service before accepting any protected administrative write, including publishing or replacing a controlled SDS, changing authoritative site/work-area chemical inventory/assignments, and granting/revoking/changing user permissions. A previously authenticated or cached offline session, including a grant now expired or revoked under D-035, must not be sufficient for these writes. A disconnected administrative device may not record an authoritative change as completed; details of optional draft-only admin work remain OPEN. **In contrast, workers retain read-only access to properly pre-synchronized, verified SDS documents and local chemical search without a current network connection or login** (D-001/D-004). Workers may also **prepare and save a chemical report locally while offline**, but the UI must clearly show **“Saved locally — not yet received”** until a server confirms receipt, and must not imply that an administrator has been notified (advances D-013). Urgent concerns continue to require direct field Safety/supervisor contact rather than relying on pending app transmission. **Rationale:** prevent stale delegated privileges from being used for record changes while preserving immediate access to chemical hazard information and practical offline field reporting. **OPEN:** report transmission/retry rules, shared-device attribution, stale document and device synchronization, offline storage privacy, and precise authorization enforcement architecture; this decision is not a production-security certification.

**CONFIRMED D-037 (2026-11-22) — Automatically transmit offline worker reports after connectivity returns, subject to identity verification and server receipt.** A worker may compose and save a chemical report while disconnected (D-036), clearly labeled **“Saved locally — not yet received”**. The system should **automatically retry sending** when connectivity and supported application execution return, rather than relying on the worker to remember to press Send again. The server must verify **the actual reporting worker's identity** before accepting a report attributed to that person, particularly on a shared device; an expired or unverifiable identity blocks submission and prompts reauthentication while retaining the local draft. Use a stable client report identifier and server-side duplicate prevention/idempotency so interrupted retries cannot create repeated administrator reports. Only after explicit server acknowledgment may the UI show **“Submitted — received by administrator”** and present a report reference/receipt; pending and failed states remain visible and actionable. **Urgent hazards always require direct Safety/supervisor contact; an offline draft or retry is not evidence of hazard notification.** **Rationale:** reduce forgotten field reports without mistaken delivery claims or identity/duplicate failures. **OPEN:** actual operating-system/browser background-sync limitations and behavior when the app is closed, secure local draft storage, identity enrollment and shared-device session handling, retry/backoff, offline media-size limits, and retention. Automatic transmission is a product goal, not a guarantee that a closed PWA can synchronize while offline or suspended.

**CONFIRMED D-038 (2026-11-22) — Flexible shared-device reporting with separate session-account and self-identified reporter attribution.** SkyFire SDS must support chemical reports from personal devices, mine-owned shared devices, and appropriately managed shared/kiosk sessions without requiring every worker to own a device or possess an individually dedicated session. On report intake, record **(a) the authenticated account or shared/kiosk session actually used, when one exists, and relevant audit context** (such as time/device reference where proportionate) and **(b) the individual whom the person submitting the report identifies as the actual reporter**. Provide an easy field to enter or correct the reporting person's name when the session belongs to another person or to a shared account; clearly mark a merely entered name as **self-reported/unverified identity** rather than as an authenticated person. Retain any mismatch for follow-up instead of silently attributing the report to the logged-in user. Audit the **account/session identifier, not passwords, authentication secrets, or raw login credentials**. Support legitimate shared-session workflows while avoiding endorsement of sharing another person's private password; security and privacy practices remain to be designed. The owner would prefer every worker to eventually have a dedicated device but explicitly does **not** make individual devices a condition for SDS access or reporting; do not introduce artificial per-user licensing friction as a default product requirement, while leaving actual hosting/identity costs OPEN. Preserve login-free approved SDS viewing, offline report drafts/confirmed server receipt, protected hazard communication, and stricter authorization for administrative writes. **D-037 supersession/clarification:** D-037's absolute statement that the actual human reporter must be independently verified *before the report can be accepted* is **superseded for shared-device intake**: the system verifies the account/session as applicable but may accept an accurately labeled, self-identified reporter, without pretending that person's identity was authenticated. The identity confidence, corroboration, and follow-up requirements remain **OPEN**; do not discard safety reports solely because personal attribution is not yet independently verified. **Rationale:** preserve useful safety reporting, attribution evidence and operational flexibility across different mine sizes and device arrangements.

**CONFIRMED D-039 (2026-11-22) — Reporter name entry on shared sessions; editable prefill on personal sessions.** For a chemical/status report submitted from a managed shared/kiosk session, prompt for the **person submitting the report** and require an entered reporter name in the ordinary digital report form. For an individually authenticated session, prefill the signed-in worker's name but allow the worker to correct it if they are reporting on behalf of someone else or the session is not theirs. Preserve D-038's separate attribution fields: retain the account/session used, a clearly **self-reported vs authenticated** identity distinction and discrepancies for administrator review. Prefilling a name must not be misrepresented as proof that the named individual acted. Keep the form lightweight and aligned with the two-action reporting intent where feasible; if needed, entry occurs in the compact confirmation step rather than adding separate workflow pages. **Rationale:** simple reporting from a single workshop device or many employee devices without false attribution. Do not conflate this ordinary form requirement with a legal requirement that miners identify themselves to raise an urgent hazard; immediate Safety/supervisor communication and other protected reporting paths must remain possible. Authentication, privacy and alternative reporting details remain OPEN.

**CONFIRMED D-040 (2026-11-22) — Administrator-configurable deployment and worker report forms.** SkyFire SDS should support **mine/site-specific administrator configuration** of shared reporting stations/devices (a single shop tablet, multiple tablets, individual devices, or a mixed arrangement), report-form fields and help text, and dropdown **display labels and appropriate choices**, to accommodate differing operation sizes and terminology without requiring code changes or imposing per-device/per-user licensing assumptions. Treat this as **configuration of the SDS reporting experience**, not authority to rewrite original SDS documents, alter company policy, or silently change the meaning of system audit states. Preserve stable underlying report/status identifiers for cross-site tracking and consistent analytics when labels differ (e.g., a site may call “Maintenance Shop” a “Workshop”); distinguish editable local terms from protected system meanings. Configuration must not disable access to verified SDSs, urgent hazard communication, traceable receipt/audit, essential report attribution, or other regulatory and safety guardrails. **Rationale:** broad adaptability and a practical setup for small mines through larger operations. **OPEN:** which fields may be mandatory/optional, which dropdown options are semantic statuses versus site-defined categories, configuration scope/inheritance, who can configure, approval and versioning of changes, offline synchronization, and how to prevent form complexity from undermining the rapid two-action report design.

**CONFIRMED D-041 (2026-11-22) — Configurable worker report forms with protected safety and audit core.** Authorized SDS administrators may tailor site- and department-specific **display wording, helpful instructions, optional questions, local work-area/category choices, and appropriately scoped dropdown options** without changing code (D-040). However, configuration may **not delete, suppress, falsify, or redefine the required core safety/traceability functions and semantics**: access to approved manufacturer SDSs without login; reporter-versus-session attribution and identity-confidence distinction where applicable (D-038/D-039); product reference or explicit **unidentified product** state; site/location context when known, with a way to report **unknown location** instead of blocking safety reports; reliable received-versus-unsent status and unique report/audit identifiers; report intake, safety triage, escalation and resolution history; and the separation of **SDS verified** from **chemical authorized for use**. Configurable labels may differ by mine but must map to stable internal status identifiers, without redefining or implying different authorization. Protect the integrity of the original manufacturer's SDS, keep urgent hazard communication routes available, and avoid making a convenience field mandatory in ways that obstruct genuine reporting or turn the two-action quick report into a lengthy questionnaire. **Rationale:** site-specific practicality without sacrificing accountability, consistent analysis, hazard access or regulatory safeguards. **OPEN:** exact immutable-versus-configurable field matrix; which site-defined fields may be required; delegated configuration authority; change review/version history; offline configuration sync; and how prior reports are displayed after future form edits. This confirms a **design boundary**, not a completed MSHA/OSHA legal compliance validation or authority to change mine HazCom policy.

**CONFIRMED D-042 (2026-11-22) — Versioned report-form configurations with preserved historical dropdown designations.** When an authorized administrator changes a published worker report form, create a new controlled configuration version for subsequent reports rather than rewriting older submissions. Preserve, for each report, the exact form version and question/choice **labels and meanings that were presented when the report was made**, including any subsequently renamed, retired or reorganized dropdown option. Administrators may rename or revise a dropdown for future reports without losing the prior designation or automatically reclassifying earlier records. Stable internal field/option identifiers and explicit historical mappings should support consistent searching, reporting and interpretation; where a change is substantively different, create a new identifier instead of silently reusing an old one with a new meaning. Any correction to an existing report is a separately audited amendment, not an overwrite of historical values. **Rationale:** allow flexible site terminology and evolving forms while protecting traceability and evidentiary integrity. **OPEN:** detailed version publishing permissions, whether drafts inherit changes, offline device sync, handling reports initiated against an earlier form version, and explicit historic-to-current category crosswalk policies. This is product design only; no app implementation or operator policy is authorized.

**CONFIRMED D-043 (2026-11-22) — Accept completed offline reports using their original form version without forcing re-entry after a configuration change.** A chemical report completed while disconnected must retain the worker's original observations, responses, option labels/identifiers and the exact report-form version under which it was created (D-042). When connectivity resumes, the product should submit and accept the report **under that original version** rather than forcing the worker to re-enter or silently remap the answers because an administrator has since renamed, retired or added a field/dropdown option. Keep automatic retry, submission identity, duplicate prevention and honest server-receipt handling in accordance with D-037/D-038; a local draft is not administrator-received until server acknowledgment. If a newer form introduces a relevant safety question or clarification, accept and flag the original report for administrator follow-up rather than discarding or deceptively altering it. Preserve the original received payload and log any later amendment or requested information separately. **Rationale:** preserve real-world field observations and low-friction reporting at mines with intermittent connectivity. **OPEN / technical validation:** acceptance behavior for malformed/corrupt or security-invalid payloads, safe legacy-form validation boundaries, backward compatibility/version retention period, file attachment limits, and actual offline synchronization capabilities. This does not override security checks or make an unreceived report proof of hazard notification; urgent hazards still require direct field communication.

**CONFIRMED D-044 (2026-11-22) — Track approved SDS revision distribution across supported digital devices and printed binders.** Publishing a newly verified/approved manufacturer SDS revision must create **distribution/update work items** for the relevant sites/work areas and their registered or managed digital access points and printed SDS binder packages/manifests. A connected, supported device should retrieve the approved revision when it next synchronizes; an offline device may continue to show its previously cached SDS but must show the document revision and **last successful synchronization** information without claiming that it has the latest available version. Actual device synchronization and document integrity must be **acknowledged/verified** before its status is recorded as updated. Printed binders must be marked **update required** and remain outstanding until a responsible person confirms the revised pages/package have been printed and physically replaced; generating a PDF or print job alone is not binder replacement confirmation. The administrator's gap/distribution dashboard should distinguish **current/confirmed, pending/offline, overdue/failed, and unknown/untracked** as appropriate, and link exceptions to a specific follow-up action without claiming that a green dashboard proves full legal HazCom compliance. Do not silently discard earlier SDS versions or erase historical revision/distribution records. **Rationale:** controlled SDS revision rollout across real mine connectivity and paper fallback, with truthful completion evidence. **OPEN:** registered device scope (especially privately owned or untracked devices), whether PDF download or verified cache integrity counts as complete, synchronization cadence/expiry, what constitutes overdue, who confirms binder replacement, escalation of materially changed hazards, retention of superseded offline copies, and actual PWA/browser background-sync limits. This is an SDS-specific distribution queue, not a committed Lynx work-management integration or a substitute for direct operator hazard communication and physical access to required information.

**CONFIRMED D-045 (2026-11-22) — Risk-based escalation for incomplete SDS revision distribution.** Track failed, pending or overdue approved SDS revision updates from D-044 with **risk-sensitive prioritization** rather than treating all document changes as equivalent. For a **routine/administrative revision**, maintain an actionable administrator distribution backlog and escalate according to subsequently defined schedules. When a revision includes a **significant new or changed chemical hazard** and distribution to an affected device, location or binder is incomplete, prominently flag the unresolved risk, promptly alert the responsible SDS/HazCom administrators and relevant supervisors, and trigger an explicit **real-world hazard communication follow-up** where warranted. Do not assume that a device notification alone reaches offline miners or substitutes for the operator's labeling, training, SDS access, or immediate safety-response responsibilities. Track affected sites/work areas, failed channels, notification/escalation attempts, assignments and resolution evidence without claiming completed delivery until it is actually confirmed. **Rationale:** avoid notification fatigue from inconsequential document housekeeping while raising the priority of uncommunicated material hazard changes. **OPEN:** who evaluates and records materiality; criteria for significant hazard change; configurable escalation deadlines/thresholds, ownership and coverage; acknowledgment/escalation pathways; and what alternate distribution measures the mine must take. This is an application workflow direction, not an invented mine-wide HazCom procedure or assertion of regulatory compliance.

## 7. Safety, security, regulatory and record integrity boundaries

CONFIRMED D-017: Do not put passwords in front of ordinary SDS viewing. MSHA Part 47 focuses on providing timely SDS access; no blanket MSHA requirement to encrypt or password-protect each SDS has been established. Administrative write access must be protected to avoid accidental/malicious record changes. Do not automatically publish site-specific detailed inventories, quantities, private attachments or restricted locations to the open internet.

CONFIRMED D-018: The system must provide exact manufacturer/product SDSs, no false confidence about unverified records, no AI-authored replacement SDSs, and original-PDF integrity. Confirm MSHA 30 CFR Part 47 requirements (written HazCom program, chemical list cross-reference, access, update and retention) and any applicable OSHA requirements as part of legal/regulatory QA before build or production claim. Electronic access does not remove the operator's training, labeling or contingency obligations. An SDS is not universally 'expired' after a fixed number of years.

WORKING D-019: Use an authoritative, backed-up management source with approved offline copies on worker devices and printable backups. Repository hosting and a working UI prototype are not in themselves secure production SDS record storage. Choose appropriate hosting, access control, backups, offline sync, change audit, media storage, and privacy controls in technical discovery. Validate SDS redistribution/licensing before broad public hosting.

## 8. Multi-site and interoperability

WORKING D-020: Support distinct mine sites and chemical use areas from the beginning (Quartette and Spirit Mountain are proposed initial examples), including an approved product potentially used at more than one location. Do not assume actual inventories or company-approved workflows exist yet.

WORKING D-021: Lynx remains preferred for inspection/incident/corrective-action/work coordination, subject to Trevor/Matt's real product commitments; do not create a competing mine work-management system from this project. SkyFire SDS handles SDS document-quality reports internally; genuine workplace hazards may later be linked to Lynx actions. Training-related changes may later link to Education. These are future integration concepts, not committed APIs.

## 9. Data objects and states — candidate schema, NOT finalized

Candidate entities: Site; Work Area; Chemical/Product (stable ID, exact name, manufacturer and identifiers); Controlled SDS Version (source, file/hash, issue/revision data and approval); Inventory Assignment (site, area, perhaps quantity later); Supplemental QR Label; Worker Report/Discrepancy (submitter, evidence, received status, review/closeout); Notification/Acknowledgment; Audit Entry; Device Sync State; Printed Binder Manifest. These are planning constructs pending minimal first-version scoping.

Suggested lifecycle: Worker report or admin draft → Pending review → Identity/document verified → Published to worker catalog → Synced to devices / added to print distribution. A rejected/duplicate submission retains a documented resolution rather than vanishing. 'Published SDS' is not automatically 'approved for use'.

## 10. Scope boundaries and unmade decisions

OPEN Q-001: Which organizational identity/roles administer chemicals? Is a named, authenticated worker report mandatory or can trusted temporary/contractor users report? How does no-login public worker access coexist with protected site data?

OPEN Q-002: Minimum verification/publish workflow: who can verify product identity/SDS authenticity, approve publication, request revision, and identify a chemical as permitted for use? Do not decide mine-wide operating policy without mine leadership.

OPEN Q-003: What does 'always available offline' mean operationally? Which sites/areas are cached, maximum library size, media/offline upload limits, re-sync intervals, device-loss response, printing/binder update procedures?

OPEN Q-004: Notifications: exact triggers, affected audiences, push vs. in-app priorities, acknowledgment obligations, supervisor escalation, and whether a chemical addition triggers training. Company site policy still needed.

OPEN Q-005: Technical hosting and vendor choices, secure central database/file store, role-based access, backup/restore, security review, retention and estimated ongoing cost. Avoid promising free enterprise hosting before evaluation.

OPEN Q-006: Product schema/permissions for site inventories, labeling details, restricted locations, external contractors, chemical disposal/record archiving; verify applicable retention/regulatory interpretations with authoritative sources.

OPEN Q-007: Define smallest usable v0.1 prototype, actual mock data, worker interface and admin interface, acceptance tests, implementation milestone, and repository governance. Do not deploy or enter mine data during early prototyping.

DEFERRED: Automatic web-wide SDS scraping, AI-generated SDS summaries, broad QR code replacements for regulated labels, purchasing workflows, full Lynx integration, SSO across SkyFire apps, server push, enterprise analytics and generalized EHS suites until separately scoped.

## 11. Required acceptance principles before eventual mine use

Use complete verified SDS PDFs; prove a worker can search and retrieve an approved SDS on iPhone and shared tablet completely offline after sync; prove QR label lookup and printed fallback; verify administrator-only editing and version audit; verify that worker drafts remain outside approved public catalog; verify exact report attribution on shared devices; verify notification targeting and correct offline/pending messages; simulate document replacement and ensure stale-device indicator and print binder update notice; test backup and recovery. Operational training, labeling and other legally required access must remain established outside the software itself.
